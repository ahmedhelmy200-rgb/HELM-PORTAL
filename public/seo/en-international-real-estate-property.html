Cross-border legal coordination

International Real Estate and Property Transactions

Location of property is central when reviewing a cross-border property transaction.

Identify property and legal right

Record where the property is situated, the official register or reference, the parties' identities and the desired act: sale, purchase, lease, title transfer, return of funds or contractual relief. The nationality of a buyer or seller does not necessarily determine the rules for every interest in the land.

Review the documents

Collect the full agreement and amendments, official property records, any powers of attorney, financing or mortgage information, payment receipts, delivery and acceptance messages and known prior proceedings. Distinguish an interest registered against land from a personal contractual obligation.

Illustrative advance payment

A buyer living in the UAE pays a deposit for a property abroad and then disputes handover. First review the seller's authority, title information, contract, handover dates, receipt and later messages. Do not assume that a bank transfer alone proves an entitlement to transfer title or recover the amount.

Local requirements and advisers

A local land registry or court can impose its own procedures and professional representation rules. Confirm the proper receiving authority and any translations or attestations. See international contracts, document attestation and commercial disputes.

Scope: General legal information only. Different jurisdictions impose distinct legal and professional requirements. Confirm current rules and involve authorized local advisers where required.
