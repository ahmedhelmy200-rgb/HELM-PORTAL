Judgment Enforcement in the UAE

Review the enforceable instrument, outstanding amount and actual case decisions before requesting enforcement measures.

Start with the enforceable instrument

Obtain the full judgment, order or instrument and confirm its procedural status under the applicable rules. Determine the parties bound by it and identify the exact operative terms. The type of instrument and the competent authority matter: a court dispute that has not yet resulted in an enforceable instrument should not be treated as an existing enforcement case.

Prepare the outstanding-balance record

Separate the principal obligation, any court-awarded ancillary amounts and the execution-related amounts that are properly applicable. Reconcile every actual payment or settlement. The current balance should be verifiable from records rather than inferred from the figure stated in the original judgment alone.

Review the current case before requesting a measure

Check which execution requests have been filed, what decisions were made, whether notices or service steps occurred, and whether money has been recovered. Measures such as attachment or travel restrictions depend on legal conditions and a decision by the competent authority; they should not be described as automatic consequences of any debt.

Illustrative example: payment after judgment

A debtor pays part of the judgment sum after the enforcement file was opened. The parties should identify the payment date, amount and what debt it was allocated toward. The next application should use the accurate remaining balance and the current case status rather than repeat the original claim unchanged. The example is hypothetical.

Related reading

For claims that have not reached the enforcement stage, see financial claims and payment orders. The official UAE legislation service should be consulted for the rules in force. Court requirements and procedural deadlines need review in the actual case.
