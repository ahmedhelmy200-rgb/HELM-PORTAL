Cross-Border Claims

International debt recovery and claim coordination

For cross-border claims, the file must identify the debt basis, supporting records, party locations, relevant assets and any prior judgments or proceedings.

Core file components

  • Contract, acknowledgment, invoice or other basis of the claim.
  • Correspondence and supporting evidence.
  • Locations of the parties and relevant assets.
  • Any prior court decision or enforcement-related record.

Multi-jurisdiction approach

We maintain one central file while separating the legal route required in each jurisdiction. Local counsel is engaged where a locally regulated act or court representation is required.

No one-size-fits-all process: the appropriate route depends on the jurisdiction, documents and procedural history.

Identify whether there is a claim or an existing enforceable decision

Debt recovery across borders begins with a classification of the file. An unpaid commercial invoice may require establishing the contractual obligation and performance. An existing judgment or other instrument may instead raise questions about recognition or enforcement in the jurisdiction where the relevant person or assets are located. These are different inquiries and should not be treated as interchangeable.

Prepare a simple chronological summary: when the transaction was agreed, what was delivered, when payment fell due, what was paid and which objections or settlements arose afterward. Do not combine unrelated invoices or transactions into a single figure without a supporting reconciliation.

Evidence checklist

  1. Correct entity: Contracts, trade details and signing authority for the debtor and creditor.
  2. Basis of the amount: Invoices, acknowledgments or agreements explaining why the amount is claimed.
  3. Performance: Documents evidencing services rendered or goods delivered, and any acceptance or rejection records.
  4. Payments: Bank confirmations, receipts and a dated ledger of payments and proposed settlements.
  5. Communications: Original email threads, relevant messages, payment demands and replies.
  6. Previous proceedings: Decisions, settlements, notifications and records of amounts already recovered.

Illustrative scenario: payment withheld after delivery

An exporter claims payment for goods delivered to a customer in another country, while the customer alleges that the final shipment was deficient. A practical first step is to match each invoice to the corresponding delivery and acceptance documents. Distinguish undisputed sums from disputed goods and record any partial payment. Only then should the contract's dispute-resolution terms, the relevant jurisdictions and possible next steps be assessed.

Do not assume a single international enforcement process

Legal procedures depend on the documents, the forum, the type of decision and the law or agreements applicable in the jurisdiction concerned. A foreign court judgment is not automatically executable everywhere simply because it is certified. A local qualified professional may be needed for the registration of proceedings or execution measures in another country.
