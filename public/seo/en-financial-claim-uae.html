Financial Claims in the UAE

A practical framework for understanding a payment dispute before selecting an application or lawsuit.

Start by identifying the obligation

Establish why the money is claimed: a contract, delivered goods, completed services, an acknowledgment, or another documented transaction. Identify the correct debtor and creditor, including whether a representative acted for a company or assumed any separate obligation. An invoice may indicate an amount requested but does not necessarily resolve disagreement about delivery or performance.

Prepare an accurate account

Create a dated reconciliation of the original amount, any agreed adjustments, payments actually received and the remaining disputed balance. Separate the claimed principal from interest, compensation and procedural expenses so that each amount has its own basis. Preserve transfer confirmations and complete communications.

Different procedural routes

Ordinary litigation may be needed where material facts or performance require examination. A payment order is a special procedural route subject to statutory conditions and required notice; it is not automatic for every invoice. If there is already an enforceable instrument, assessment may begin at the enforcement stage instead. The appropriate forum and process depend on the relevant UAE legislation and the documents.

Compare the payment order guide with our judgment enforcement guide.

Illustrative example

A supplier sends three invoices, but the customer claims part of the third delivery was incomplete and has paid two installments. The useful starting point is a document set linking each invoice to the contract, delivery evidence, objections and payments, followed by a clear calculation of the remaining disputed amount. This hypothetical example does not establish either party's legal position.

Checklist

  1. Agreement and amendments, or other basis of the obligation.
  2. Correct parties, identities, trade records and signing authority where relevant.
  3. Proof of delivery or completion and related objections.
  4. Invoice schedule and receipt or transfer records.
  5. Notices, settlements, and any prior court decisions or proceedings.

For electronically recorded payments and admissions, see the evidence guide.
