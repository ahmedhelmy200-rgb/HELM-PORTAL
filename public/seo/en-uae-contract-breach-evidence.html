Proving Breach of Contract in the UAE

Start from the signed obligation, compare performance to the agreed standard and distinguish unpaid sums from damages.

Work from the complete agreed contract

Collect the signed agreement, accepted offer, purchase orders, annexes, any scope changes and related correspondence. Identify the exact duty alleged to have been breached: specification, quantity, deadline, payment, notice or required approval. Check the party responsible for performance and any condition precedent relevant to that obligation.

Document what actually happened

Build a comparison table with the contractual duty, due date, evidence of performance, actual result and claimed deviation. Include delivery reports, acceptance messages, quality comments and proof of partial completion. A task that was never delivered, a delivered item alleged to be defective and a scope later changed by agreement are different factual scenarios.

Why notices and communications matter

The contract may establish procedures for requesting corrections, granting further time, objecting or terminating. Preserve the full record of notices, who received them and when. Informal exchanges can still be relevant, but should be read in context with the signed obligations and the applicable law.

Debt balance versus compensation

A claim for the remainder of a contract price is conceptually different from compensation for a specific alleged loss. Organize each sum independently, its contractual or legal basis, supporting records and the calculation used. A demand for compensation needs a properly assessed legal and factual foundation; do not treat every inconvenience as recoverable harm by default.

Illustrative project dispute

A supplier delivers two phases of a three-phase project and later demands the full contractual price. The customer claims that the second phase required correction. Determine what milestones were originally specified, what was delivered and accepted, what changed by later agreement, and what was paid. The next legal step depends on that evidence and the rules in force. More guidance: contract drafting and review, commercial disputes and financial claims.

Important: This guide explains general legal information. The correct route and legal consequence depend on the facts, documents, current applicable legislation and competent authority. The examples are hypothetical.
