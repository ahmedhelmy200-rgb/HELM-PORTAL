How to prepare for a UAE legal consultancy review

A useful first consultation starts with a clear account of the underlying transaction, not with a conclusion about who is right. Identify the parties and their legal capacities, the underlying contract or transaction, the relevant dates, the documents available and the precise outcome being requested. Whether a matter concerns unpaid invoices, an employment disagreement, a contractual dispute or judgment enforcement, the documents and procedural stage affect which questions must be answered first.

Financial claims and commercial disputes

For a commercial claim, collect the agreement and amendments, invoices, delivery or completion records, payment confirmations and correspondence relating to any objections or settlement. Prepare a dated ledger that distinguishes the original amount claimed, amounts actually paid and the remaining balance. A bank transfer confirms movement of funds; it does not, by itself, establish why the money was paid or whether repayment is due.

Where the parties disagree about whether goods or services were properly delivered, the relevant question may be proof of performance rather than just the value of an invoice. Read the Arabic guide to financial claim routes in the UAE and the guide to messages and bank transfer evidence.

Contracts, employment and enforcement

When reviewing a contract, check party identity, scope of work, measurable deliverables, payment conditions, notice requirements, termination mechanisms and dispute resolution terms. For employment-related files, organize the employment contract, payroll records, the termination documents and any administrative or judicial correspondence. For a judgment, distinguish reviewing the underlying dispute from reviewing whether the existing order is enforceable and which procedural applications are appropriate.

Practical starting points include contract performance and breach, labour disputes, and judgment enforcement. The substantive articles are currently available in Arabic.

Illustrative scenario: unpaid services with a partial dispute

Suppose an UAE-based supplier submits three invoices, but the customer maintains that the final phase was never accepted. Instead of assuming every invoice is immediately recoverable, review the original scope, completion evidence, acceptance process, objections and partial payments. Create a schedule of agreed and disputed items. Only then can the appropriate procedural route be evaluated against the currently applicable law and the competent forum.
