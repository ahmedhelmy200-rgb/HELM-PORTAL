Legal Consultancy for Matters in Ajman

Separate a new debt claim from a payment-order application and an existing execution file before selecting the next step.

Three different procedural starting points

A party may need to prove a right in ordinary litigation, may consider the special payment-order route if statutory conditions are met, or may already have an enforceable instrument allowing an execution request. These routes are not interchangeable. Start by examining the origin, maturity, documentary support and amount of the claimed obligation, then check whether an enforceable decision or instrument already exists.

Payment-order considerations

A signed acknowledgment or invoice does not automatically make the payment-order procedure appropriate. Review the document creating the debt, the legal requirements for the nature of the claim and any demand for payment or contested issue. The actual procedural requirements and competent court must be confirmed under the rules in force.

Execution, attachments and travel restrictions

In an existing execution case, distinguish the original judgment amount, part-payments, fees and current balance. Obtain the actual judicial decisions concerning restrictions or attachments and check notices, relevant objections and prior requests. A debt alone does not demonstrate that a travel ban or attachment has been issued.

Instalment arrangements and settlement

If a debtor has made payments or sought instalments, record each transfer and its purpose. Review whether a written agreement simply organizes repayment or changes the original obligation. Filing an instalment request does not by itself prove judicial acceptance; any decision must be read directly.

Illustrative debt acknowledgment

Suppose a debt acknowledgment is followed by multiple bank transfers and a later installment agreement. Compile a dated account of the initial obligation, amounts paid, adjustments and the remaining balance. Include earlier judicial orders and the outcome sought: payment, instalments, correction of balance or review of a measure. See payment orders, travel restrictions and execution procedures.
