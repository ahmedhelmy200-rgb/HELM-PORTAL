Cheque Enforcement in the UAE

The bank's reason for returning a cheque and the underlying transaction may affect the available legal route.

Start with the cheque and bank record

Gather the cheque or legally acceptable copy, the bank's official return statement and the exact wording explaining why payment was declined. The legal treatment of a cheque and whether a direct execution route is available depend on the governing UAE commercial law and applicable judicial principles. Not every returned cheque should be placed automatically into one procedural category.

Why the return reason matters

Reasons such as the absence or insufficiency of available funds require careful distinction from other banking or documentary issues. The original Arabic guide also discusses judicial principles concerning an account closure in the context of cheque enforceability. Any such principle must be checked against the official decision and current law when used for an actual filing.

An executable instrument is not necessarily the full story

Even where an enforcement route is available, the cheque may have arisen from a sale, deposit, guarantee or settlement. The underlying relationship and subsequent payments can become important in a substantive execution dispute. Do not infer that a bank return statement finally decides all questions about the parties' underlying obligations.

Preparation checklist

  1. Preserve the cheque, identifying details and the bank's official response.
  2. Record the reason for return verbatim, without substituting a different description.
  3. Gather the contract, invoice or settlement that explains the cheque's issuance.
  4. Reconcile all part-payments and later arrangements against the amount claimed.
  5. Check whether any previous enforcement request, objection or judicial decision exists.

Illustrative transaction

A business provides a cheque connected to a supplier agreement, but later contests the quantity supplied. Before proposing any execution response, distinguish the cheque, the bank return reason, delivery records, settlement communications and the actual balance.
