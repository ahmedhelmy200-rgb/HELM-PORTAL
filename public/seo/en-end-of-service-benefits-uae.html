End-of-Service Benefits in the UAE

A reliable assessment starts with the correct employment regime, period of service and the applicable definition of wage.

Check eligibility and the employment regime

Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 provides end-of-service rules for relevant UAE private-sector employment relationships. In the traditional system, Article 51 addresses gratuity for eligible foreign full-time employees who have completed at least one year of continuous service. Relevant exclusions, particular employment arrangements and alternative systems should be checked before making a calculation.

Traditional gratuity calculation

Under the traditional regime described in Article 51, the calculation generally uses twenty-one days of basic wage for each of the first five years and thirty days for years beyond five, subject to the statutory conditions and maximum limits. Eligible partial years are treated according to the applicable rules. The last basic wage and periods excluded from qualifying service must be verified. The calculation should not simply apply the daily rate to total remuneration including allowances.

When are outstanding employment amounts payable?

Article 53 addresses the employer's obligation to pay the worker's wages and other entitlements within fourteen days from the contract's end, subject to the applicable framework. Separate the gratuity from unpaid wages, leave balances and any other valid contractual or legal claim, with documents and arithmetic for each amount.

The alternative savings scheme

The UAE has introduced an optional alternative end-of-service savings system for participating employers and workers. Whether the worker is under a traditional gratuity arrangement or an approved alternative scheme must be verified before using a single formula. Do not assume all employers have opted into the alternative arrangement.

Document checklist and illustration

Prepare the employment contract, appointment and termination dates, basic wage history, payroll records, unpaid leave and any scheme participation or payment evidence. For example, two employees with the same gross monthly remuneration may have different qualifying basic wages and employment arrangements; their calculations should not be presumed identical. See labour disputes and current guidance from MOHRE.
