Cross-Border Inheritance and Estates: Document Planning

Organize estate assets and succession documents separately for each jurisdiction.

Establish the persons and documents

Identify the deceased and heirs with accurate official records and collect available death, family relationship, will, succession or judicial documents where relevant. The source guide stresses initial documentary organization rather than assuming substantive entitlement.

Locate the assets

Create a country-by-country inventory of property, financial accounts, business interests and supporting ownership documents. An asset's location can affect the competent authority, applicable process and documentation for that particular property.

Procedural separation

Requirements for probate, succession, ownership changes, powers of attorney and recognition of foreign documents differ between jurisdictions. Obtain advice from appropriately authorized local professionals where required. Check translation and authentication rules for the receiving body. See family-status files and powers of attorney. No inheritance share or outcome can be calculated from this general article.

Scope: General legal information only. Different jurisdictions impose distinct legal and professional requirements. Confirm current rules and involve authorized local advisers where required.
