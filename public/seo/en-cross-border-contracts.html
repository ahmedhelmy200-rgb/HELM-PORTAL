Cross-Border Contracts

International contract review

Reviewing a cross-border contract means testing how the document works in practice across jurisdictions, not just checking wording.

Key review areas

  • Correct legal identity and capacity of each party.
  • Place of performance and delivery.
  • Evidence and documentation requirements.
  • Term, renewal and termination.
  • Governing law, jurisdiction or arbitration clauses.
Where local law issues arise: locally licensed counsel can be engaged for jurisdiction-specific review where required.

Start with the transaction map

A cross-border agreement can involve one party registered in the UAE, another party overseas and performance in a third location. Record where each obligation is performed, where delivery occurs, which entity actually signs and pays, and how acceptance or non-performance will be documented. The place of signature alone does not resolve every governing-law or jurisdiction question.

Check that the person signing has authority to bind the correct legal entity. A manager's name in email correspondence is not necessarily evidence of a personal guarantee. Where individual undertakings are contemplated, they require careful separate review.

Five issues to review before execution

  1. Parties and scope: Full legal names, company capacity, exact services or goods and measurable deliverables.
  2. Evidence and acceptance: Delivery documents, testing periods, agreed quality standards, notice routes and how an objection is recorded.
  3. Payment: Currency, milestone dates, acceptance dependencies, expenses, banking details and reconciliation of partial payments.
  4. Changes and exit: Written variations, schedule changes, consequences of delay, termination process and steps for returning or preserving relevant records.
  5. Dispute resolution: Governing law, court or arbitration wording, language and the practical forum for the parties involved, subject to applicable mandatory rules.

Illustrative scenario: a software implementation contract

Company A commissions a three-stage implementation from Company B. After the second stage, A requests additional features while B claims the agreed scope has been completed. Before assessing payment or termination, identify the signed scope, acceptance criteria, change requests and milestone evidence. Separate original contractual duties from new requests and document any revised timetable. The example illustrates the file-analysis process only; it does not determine legal liability.

What to provide for review

Prepare the complete agreement, relevant schedules and amendments, purchase orders, correspondence, delivery evidence, payment records and a timeline of the disagreement. Identify the jurisdictions involved and any proceedings already commenced. For confidentiality, exchange sensitive documents only through authorized professional channels.
