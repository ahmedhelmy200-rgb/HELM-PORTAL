Arbitration in the UAE

Read the precise arbitration agreement and governing rules before choosing court or arbitration proceedings.

The arbitration agreement and its scope

Federal Law No. 6 of 2018 concerning Arbitration provides the central federal framework, subject to its amendments and relevant rules. Determine whether the arbitration agreement is in writing, which parties are bound, which disputes it covers, whether the institution or rules are identified and the agreed seat, language and other procedural arrangements.

An objection before the court

Article 8 addresses the effect of an arbitration agreement when a covered dispute is brought before a court, including the importance of how and when the defendant relies on the agreement and circumstances in which the agreement is invalid or incapable of performance. Raising an arbitration point late or without reading the complete clause can affect the procedural position.

Tribunal and proceedings

Review any provisions on the appointment and number of arbitrators, alongside the legal requirements governing the tribunal's composition, independence and impartiality. A request for particular interim relief or a question about proceedings before a court requires an analysis of the relevant legal powers, rather than a general assumption that all courts are excluded once arbitration is agreed.

Award, challenge and enforcement

An arbitral award may lead to separate questions about recognition, execution or an application challenging its validity within the grounds and procedures prescribed by law. A foreign award may also involve applicable treaties, such as the New York Convention when its requirements are met. The location of the award and the jurisdiction where enforcement is sought remain important.

Checklist

  1. Obtain the full agreement and any incorporated standard terms.
  2. Identify the parties and the scope of the alleged dispute.
  3. Read the arbitration seat, institution, rules, language and appointment provisions.
  4. Check current procedural steps and the timing of any objection.
  5. Gather prior notices, correspondence and tribunal or court decisions.
