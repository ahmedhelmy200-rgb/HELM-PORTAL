Drafting and Reviewing Contracts in the UAE

A sound contract should make the parties' obligations measurable and the evidence of performance clear.

Identify the parties and their legal capacity

Review the full legal names of the contracting parties, the company records where relevant, and the authority of each signatory. Distinguish a company undertaking from a personal guarantee; one should not be presumed from the appearance of a director's name. Verify which schedules, purchase orders and accepted quotations form part of the agreement.

Scope, performance and payment

The contract should say exactly what is delivered, to what specification, at which location and when. Establish an acceptance process and a record of defects, variations or revisions. Review the amount, currency, installment conditions, dates of maturity, supporting invoices and what constitutes valid evidence of payment. Broad descriptions without measurable deliverables are common sources of avoidable disputes.

Breach, termination and allocation of risk

Review the circumstances treated as default, notice methods, cure periods where agreed, the consequences of termination and any proposed liability limits. Consider whether the provisions are balanced and consistent with the applicable law. A contractual penalty or compensation provision should not be described as automatically enforceable without analysis.

Dispute resolution and evidential records

Verify the intended court or arbitration route and any mandatory rules affecting jurisdiction. Record the addresses and channels for notices, changes, acceptance, complaints and communications. The objective is to make each material contractual obligation verifiable through a dated document or message.

Example and preparation checklist

In a hypothetical design project, the supplier claims full fees while the customer alleges that a later design phase was incomplete. The review compares each agreed milestone, delivery evidence, approval or objection and any revised scope. It separates payment for completed work from a claim for damages. Bring the complete agreement, annexes, payment ledger, relevant messages and a timeline of disputed milestones.
