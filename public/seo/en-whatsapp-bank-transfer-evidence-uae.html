WhatsApp and Bank Transfers as Evidence

A transfer can establish that money moved; assessing why it moved requires the surrounding relationship and communications.

Start with the underlying transaction

Before examining screenshots or bank statements, identify the legal relationship between the parties. Was the payment connected to a loan, sale, investment, deposit, wages or services? An electronic message may help explain the parties' intentions, but its meaning should be read within the full conversation and the other available documents.

Bank evidence and messages should be assessed together

Record the payer and recipient, dates, amounts and reference details. Match each transfer to the contract or request that allegedly explains it. Preserve full message threads, including relevant replies and later settlements, rather than selecting isolated extracts that change the context.

Evidence checklist

  1. The agreement, acknowledgment or clear record explaining the payment's purpose.
  2. Official banking confirmations or account records when available.
  3. Complete and dated electronic conversations relevant to the claimed obligation.
  4. Evidence of repayment, partial performance, settlement or any counter-objection.
  5. Information needed to verify authorship, integrity and context where the evidence is disputed.

Illustrative example

A person transfers money to a business and later asks for its return. One message refers to repayment, while another refers to a possible purchase. The relevant assessment cannot stop at the transfer receipt or a single screenshot. The entire transaction history, the parties' agreed purpose and subsequent messages must be reviewed before characterizing the obligation.
